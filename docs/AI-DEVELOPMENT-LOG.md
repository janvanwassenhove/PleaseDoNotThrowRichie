## Iteration 3 — Hosting and releases

Date: 2026-09-18. Goal: make the game reachable without a checkout, and cut releases the way the other games do. AI/tool: Claude Code. Generated: a GitHub Pages deploy workflow, a release workflow that bumps, tags, screenshots, zips the site and publishes notes, an artifact cleanup workflow, a Playwright screenshot script and a `window.__richie` debug hook. Observed problems: the HUD stayed up over the opening cinematic; the chase camera lerps too slowly to settle on a software renderer, so captures framed the wrong thing; a charge held before a reset survived it; a failed boot showed an unexplained black page. Human decision: keep the screenshots deterministic by driving game state directly rather than synthesising input. Changes: one `play()` entry point that clears every overlay, a shared chase-camera target the debug hook can snap to, `reset()` clears the charge, and a boot overlay that reports WebGL and start-up failures. Result: the built site deploys to GitHub Pages on every push to main, and each push publishes a release carrying seven captures and the playable build.

## Iteration 5 — A living venue

Date: 2026-09-18. Goal: a skippable opening, mobile play as a PWA, auditorium 8 laid out the Kinepolis way, robots and Richie at reference quality with a life of their own, and a crowd. AI/tool: Claude Code. Generated: `src/robots.ts` (patrolling, reacting Voxxy/Droid/Biggy with clearcoat shells, emissive eyes and gaits), `src/people.ts` (vertex-coloured walkers, seated audience, cheering front rows), the raked twelve-tier keynote room with curved seat rows and standing front tiers, touch controls that press the same key codes as the keyboard, a PWA manifest and precaching service worker, icon rendering via Playwright. Observed problems: the sun light aimed at the world origin, so lighting drifted along the level; seated legs stuck straight out until the legs got a knee; a `.touch` class shared between the title note and `<body>` hid the whole page on short screens; the phone probe tapped HOP before the game had started and misreported the button as dead; pointer capture can throw under emulated touch and was called before the key was registered. Human decision: keep input as one path so the physics never knows the device. Changes: the sun's target follows Richie, legs bend at the knee, the note class is `tnote`, capture is guarded and ordered after the press. Result: the venue moves — robots patrol and look up when Richie lands, people wander and sit and cheer — and the game installs on a phone with on-screen controls.

## Iteration 6 — The head spring

Date: 2026-09-18. Goal: make a charge visible on Richie himself — the head sinks while Space is held and pops out on the hop, to a limit, still attached. AI/tool: Claude Code. Generated: a damped spring on the head node's local Y (k=90, c=9, sub-stepped at 20 ms so a slow frame cannot blow it up), a target of −45 mm scaled by the charge while Space is down, an upward impulse on every launch (hop, Voxxy throw, Biggy hit), and hard stops at −45/+50 mm. Observed problem: the headless software renderer runs this scene at a few frames a second, so live samples could not resolve the pop; the spring was verified by simulating the same maths at 60 fps (peak +49.5 mm at 0.15 s, settled by 0.8 s) and the dip by a close-up render. Result: charging reads on the robot, not just the bar.
