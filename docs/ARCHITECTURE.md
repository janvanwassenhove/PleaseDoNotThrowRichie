# Architecture

The browser application uses TypeScript, Vite, Three.js and Rapier. A fixed 60 Hz physics step owns Richie's dynamic rigid body. Rendering follows it through a smoothed third-person camera. Visual and collision geometry are separate.

Positive Z is progression. Registration and exhibition are at y=0, the staircase landing at y=6, and the cinema level at y=13. Auditorium 8 follows the Kinepolis plan: you come in at the top and the room rakes down to the stage over twelve stepped tiers of curved seat rows (each tier is a box collider, each seat block one more), with the front three tiers standing room and the stage at y=6.4. These measurements compress the venue into readable gameplay. Eight proximity checkpoints prevent long repetition.

Richie moves only by physical hops. Space builds charge; release sets an upward and camera-relative directional velocity. His head rides a damped spring on the real neck pivot: holding Space pulls it down into the body in proportion to the charge, the hop kicks it up to a hard stop 50 mm (model units) above rest, and it settles within a second — the shell never leaves the body. Weak air impulses allow corrections. A downward ray detects ground while excluding Richie's collider. When tilted on the floor, recovery assistance returns the body upright after roughly one second.

Voxxy holds Richie without gravity and releases a calculated ballistic velocity. Biggy converts charge into a higher assisted launch. Droid's third recline launches Richie over the auditorium route. The standing front rows wait politely until Richie lands on them, then go wild and relay him toward the stage. Landing on the stage starts the grand finale: the three robots appear in the wings and run to Richie, then dance around him (Voxxy laps him, Droid takes the floor, Biggy spins), confetti falls, the spotlights sweep and cycle colour, Richie bounces with his antennas up, and the camera sweeps the stage for nine seconds before the results card, which is translucent so the party carries on behind it. A restart puts everyone back on patrol.

Richie is the one non-procedural asset: `src/assets/richie.glb`, built by `scripts/build-richie.py` from the official Reachy Mini description (see `docs/ASSET-SOURCES.md`). It loads through GLTFLoader at 3x life size, hangs 0.44 below the rigid body's origin so the base sits on the collider's underside, and exposes `head`, `antenna_left` and `antenna_right` nodes that the render loop animates. Voxxy, Droid and Biggy (`src/robots.ts`) are procedural models of the Robot Games model sheets, and kinematic. They are built with a small modelling kit (`src/kit.ts`): every primitive is baked into its parent's space and merged per parent and material, so a robot of a few hundred parts draws in a few dozen calls and only the joints that move are separate objects. Voxxy is clear-coated plastic over lathe profiles resampled by arc length, with seams, badge, visor eyes and forearm band drawn to canvas textures; Droid and Biggy wear generated weathered-paint maps that double as bump maps, and Biggy's belly is composed on a canvas (rust, then seams, then the stencilled badge). Behaviour: each patrols a loop of waypoints with a gait, turns to look at Richie when he is near and reacts when he lands close by; the game borrows them for the assists (Voxxy holds Richie above its own position, Biggy backs up while charging and slams forward on release). The crowd (`src/people.ts`) is vertex-coloured geometry on one shared material: walkers are five meshes each with a walk cycle, the seated audience is a single merged mesh, and the cheering front rows are four swaying clusters. Security guards (`Guard` in `src/people.ts`) are a small state machine — patrol, alert, chase, grab, throw, return — over the same walker body with a uniform, a SECURITY shirt and a vision cone drawn on the floor. Seeing Richie means: catchable (playing, not held by a helper), inside range and half-angle, and a Rapier ray from the guard's eyes that hits Richie's collider before anything else, so booths and walls hide him. A catch parks Richie in the guard's hands for a second, then a ballistic throw sends him to the previous checkpoint, which he loses; checkpoint gains are suppressed for three seconds so the flight back cannot re-award it. Lighting uses a room environment map for reflections plus one shadow-casting key light that follows Richie.

The building itself is `src/venue.ts`. `main.ts` still owns the route and its colliders; the venue module owns what it looks like: the generated textures (`src/textures.ts` picks up every image under `src/assets/textures` by name, and a missing image falls back to a flat colour), box UVs rescaled to metres so one material tiles evenly on boxes of any size, the six exhibition booths (back wall, side wall and counter are the colliders; poster, fascia, gadgets, track spots and a Duke standee are dressing), the reception desk, pillars, the cove-lit ceiling island and LED rings of the exhibition hall, the staircase's lit nosings and chrome rails, the foyer bar with its popcorn machine and sponsor lightboxes, the corridor's LED lines, and auditorium 8's acoustic walls, red step lights, velvet seats, keynote screen, lighting truss and stage tabs. Walls and ceilings are single-sided planes facing inwards, so a chase camera that drifts outside the room looks straight through them; ceilings, trusses and fittings are baked by a second kit that casts no shadows. The light is graded by where the camera is — a bright hall, a warm foyer, a dim corridor, a dark room with a lit stage — by easing the hemisphere and key lights and the fog colour between four settings. A bloom pass (half-float, 4x MSAA) makes fittings, lightboxes and robot eyes read as sources; touch devices and `?nofx` skip it. Two rules keep bloom honest: nothing lit goes below roughness 0.1, because a mirror-sharp highlight overflows a half-float buffer, and Richie's decimated mesh has its zero-length normals patched at load, because a NaN pixel is one black dot without bloom and a black rectangle with it.

Input is one path: touch buttons press the same key codes the keyboard does and the stick feeds the same aim vector as WASD, so `step()` has no notion of the device. The build is a PWA (vite-plugin-pwa): the service worker precaches every asset, wasm and GLB included. No runtime network connection, account or backend is needed.

The build is static and uses relative asset paths, so the same `dist/` runs from a domain root, a GitHub Pages project subpath or a local folder served over HTTP. A boot overlay in `index.html` stays up until the first rendered frame and rewrites itself with a readable message if WebAssembly or WebGL 2 is unavailable, instead of leaving a black page.

The two escalators beside the grand staircase run: their tread and handrail textures scroll, and while Richie is standing on one the physics step takes over his velocity along the slope and carries him to the cinema level. Riding is the alternative to hopping twenty-four steps; a hop leaves the treads, because the carry needs contact.

The music is one track — mITy.John's *Synaptic Drift* — streamed from an audio element rather than decoded into a buffer, because three minutes of 44.1 kHz stereo is about 65 MB of float32 that a phone would rather not hold. `MusicDirector` keeps the shape the procedural version had: the same `menu`, `tutorial` and `game` themes, now choosing how loud the track sits rather than what it plays, and the same two buttons. No browser starts audio before a gesture, so the first `pointerdown` or `keydown` anywhere starts it; the render loop eases the volume towards its theme's level, towards a duck while the pause card is up, or to nothing when it is switched off. `M` toggles it, and the choice is remembered in `localStorage`. The sound effects stay oscillators on the shared `AudioContext`.

Escape pauses a run and puts up a card that resumes or returns to the title; the HUD's MENU button does the same where there is no keyboard, and the results card offers the way back too. A pause stops the physics step rather than the render loop, so the venue keeps moving behind the card while the keynote clock, Richie and the guards do not.

The title screen offers a sixty-second briefing (`state==='tutorial'`; the words are in `src/tutorial.ts`): eight cards — the situation, Richie, Voxxy, Biggy, Droid, the exhibition floor, security, the finish — each over a live shot of whoever it is about. The venue keeps running underneath: the camera orbits the actual robot as it patrols, sits behind a real guard so his vision cone is in frame, and frames a real stand for the exhibitors' card, which also shows the posters and Duke in the card itself. Physics steps during the briefing so Richie rests on the floor, and on his own card he turns to the camera and demonstrates on a loop: the head sinks through a charge, he hops, lands badly and is helped upright. In landscape the subject is pushed right of centre, clear of the text. Arrow keys, Space or Enter page through it, Escape returns to the title, and the last card starts the game.

`window.__richie` exposes a debug hook over the same state the game loop owns: state and pose readers, starting and skipping the opening, opening the briefing at a card, warping to a checkpoint, snapping the chase camera, triggering a hop, framing a robot from the front and forcing the end card. The release screenshots drive that hook rather than synthesising input, which keeps captures deterministic on a software renderer.
